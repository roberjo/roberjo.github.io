01 · About

From hand-written HTML to enterprise AWS, and back to the mainframe

I started in 1999 building a website for a research department at Auburn University, then spent seven years at AT&T writing C/C++ for data mining at a scale nobody called "big data" yet. Change management at Aflac, .NET modernization at GDIT, and agency work at Kelsey Advertising & Design followed. Since 2013 I've been at Regions Financial, where I design and deliver enterprise AWS solutions for a Fortune 500 bank.

Lately I point that experience at getting real leverage out of LLMs: agents that turn vulnerability tickets into reviewed pull requests, tooling that teaches AI coding agents to use the tools teams already rely on, and modern engineering wrapped around the systems most people would rather not touch, like mainframe COBOL batch.

  • Ship it tested. CI, golden files, and end-to-end tests are part of the design, not an afterthought.
  • Make it visible. If nobody can see into a system, it isn't done.
  • Spend deliberately. Many of my projects run entirely on free tiers, by design.

03 · Architecture

How a vulnerability becomes a pull request

ticket-remediation in motion: two scheduled pipelines, a shared Jira board, and a pluggable LLM that reads the repository and writes the fix. State lives in SQLite, so re-runs never create duplicates.
